Call For Tutorials

The AJCAI 2026 Organising Committee invites proposals for tutorials to be presented at the Australasian Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence (AJCAI 2026), which will be held in Auckland, New Zealand, from 27 to 30 November 2026.

This year, the AJCAI tutorial program will focus primarily on three major areas of artificial intelligence:

  • Machine Learning (ML)
  • Natural Language Processing (NLP)
  • Computer Vision (CV)

We welcome proposals covering fundamental concepts, recent advances, emerging research challenges, and practical applications in these areas. Tutorials addressing connections across ML, NLP, and CV, such as foundation models, multimodal learning, vision-language models, generative AI, and embodied or agentic AI, are particularly encouraged.

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Machine learning theory and algorithms
  • Deep learning and representation learning
  • Foundation models and generative AI
  • Large language models and language agents
  • Natural language understanding and generation
  • Information retrieval and retrieval-augmented generation
  • Computer vision and visual recognition
  • Image and video understanding and generation
  • Vision-language and multimodal learning
  • Reinforcement learning and agentic AI
  • Efficient learning, adaptation, and model deployment
  • Trustworthy, responsible, explainable, and fair AI
  • AI robustness, privacy, security, and safety
  • Applications of ML, NLP, and CV in science, healthcare, education, industry, and society

Proposals on other timely AI topics may also be considered, provided that they are relevant to the AJCAI community.

Tutorials should provide a balanced, accessible, and representative overview of the selected topic. Presenters are encouraged to cover the broader research landscape rather than focus primarily on their own research results, products, or tools.

Each tutorial should be 1.5 to 3 hours long and should normally be delivered by one or two presenters.

Proposal Requirements

Tutorial proposals should include the following information:

  • Title
  • Brief description and outline. Describe the topic and scope of the tutorial. Please provide a structured outline of the material to be covered, including an indicative time allocation for each section and relevant references.
  • Goals and learning outcomes. Explain the objectives of the tutorial and what participants are expected to learn from it.
  • Relevance to AJCAI 2026. Explain how the proposed tutorial relates to Machine Learning, Natural Language Processing, Computer Vision, or their intersections, and why the topic is timely and relevant to the AJCAI community.
  • Target audience. Identify the intended audience, the expected number of participants, and any prerequisite knowledge or background.
  • Tutorial format and duration. Indicate the proposed duration of the tutorial, which should be between 1.5 and 3 hours. Please describe the planned format, including lectures, demonstrations, hands-on activities, or audience discussions where applicable.
  • Presenters. Provide the names, affiliations, and email addresses of all presenters, together with brief biographies and descriptions of their expertise in the tutorial area. Tutorials should normally have no more than two presenters. For proposals involving two presenters, please indicate how the presentation time will be divided. Where available, please include links to slides, recordings, or other materials from relevant previous presentations.
  • Previous and related tutorials. List any related or similar tutorials presented by the proposers or others during the past three years. Please provide the dates and venues and explain the similarities and differences between those tutorials and the proposed tutorial.

Selection Criteria

Tutorial proposals will be evaluated based on:

  • Relevance to ML, NLP, CV, or their intersections
  • Timeliness and importance of the proposed topic
  • Clarity and quality of the proposed content
  • Suitability for the intended audience
  • Presenter expertise and teaching experience
  • Breadth and representativeness of the material
  • Potential interest to the AJCAI community
  • Diversity of topics and presenters across the tutorial program
